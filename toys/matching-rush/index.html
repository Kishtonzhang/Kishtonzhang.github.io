Background: Why does "rushing" exist?

In real hiring markets, key information (future potential, adaptability, long-term performance) takes time to surface. But firms facing competition can't wait — they make early offers based only on observable quality (resumes, grades), locking in candidates before fully understanding their true value. Meanwhile, risk-averse candidates face a dilemma: accept a sure offer now, or gamble on better ones later?

This simulation quantifies how much efficiency is lost when all players act rationally in this asymmetric-information game.

How Rushing Harms Market Efficiency

1. Information Asymmetry: Rush firms only see observable quality; the unobservable future potential portion is .

2. Risk-Aversion Driven Acceptance: Candidates who lack confidence accept early offers even when their true value ranking is higher.

3. Cascading Displacement: One wrong lock displaces the optimal candidate from their slot, and the ripple effect propagates through the entire market.

4. Concentration Shifts Who Rushes: In dispersed markets everyone competes; in concentrated markets, small firms rush while giants stay calm.
